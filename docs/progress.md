## 2026-11-24: fluency and misconceptions (0.12.0)

- Asked for: the next spec gap, answer speed (fluency) and misconceptions read from wrong answers.
- Fluency feeds nothing: §10 says never to treat speed as mastery without controlling for modality, motor ability, device and fatigue. It is reported per skill with those controls (same kind and step, the learner against themself, early in a session, pauses left out), and no state, estimate or engine choice reads it. Speed was already used beside accuracy in the engagement patterns (0.8.0), which is where the spec's §11 table puts it.
- Misconceptions are stored, not recomputed: the round is rebuilt to judge an attempt anyway, so its traps are recorded then. A later change to a pack (which makes the round unrebuildable) or to the rules cannot rewrite what a tap showed, and the engine's replay reads exactly what the decision saw.
- Chance, not counts: a wrong tap has to land somewhere, and in a two-tile round it always lands on the one wrong option. So a misconception is suspected only when its taps beat what a random wrong tap would give (a Poisson binomial tail under 0.05), which a two-tile round can never do alone.
- Mastery: a suspected misconception caps its skills at Emerging (a reporting state, derived), and moves no BKT estimate, since its answers were already counted wrong. It lifts when cleared.
- Engine: `utility@5` adds a probe bonus and searches seeds from a generator of its own for a round offering the trap, so the main draw (which kind, the second offer's seed) is the same as without it and a replay finds the same seed.

## 2026-11-23: offline answers (0.9.0)

- Asked for: the owner approved offline answer saving (spec §14): an answer the server did not receive was lost. It had to keep the append-only log, first-answer-only evidence, pack versions on each attempt and exact engine replay.
- What made it small on the server: attempts were already idempotent by `event_id`, judged by rebuilding the round from (kind, version, choices, seed, packs), ordered by when they were shown, and decisions replay from what had been received by their time. So an answer that lands late is judged exactly as one that lands at once, a resend is acknowledged as a duplicate, and a decision made while it waited is remade without it. The test plays an engine session through an offline stretch (one decision made while answers waited, one after), sends one answer twice, and replays all three decisions exactly.
- Rounds while offline: the server must still own the truth, so the browser never makes or judges a round; it keeps rounds the server made (`GET /api/rounds`, a batch so it is one request every few rounds) and shows the answer key the round already carries, as it does online. A kept round is played once. A fresh round whose template or pack versions differ shows the server moved on, and the kept rounds of that kind are dropped.
- The engine cannot decide offline: each decision follows from the answer before it, and making several ahead would decide without those answers. So "Chosen for them" falls back on kept rounds of the kinds the engine chose that visit, recorded as the device's choice (`asked: "offline"`, a new value) in a sitting of their own, never against a decision, and asks the engine for the decision it was waiting for after each round. A round the device chose offline that names a decision is refused.
- Refused or waiting: a 4xx is the server's answer for good (a pack changed, a conflicting resend) and is listed with its reason; anything else, including a login in front of the app answering 401/403, waits. A sign-in that expired must never drop answers.
- Common code: the queue and its status line are what any app writing in the field needs, so they went to strata-react (PR #308, `createOutbox`, `OutboxStatus`); `web/src/outbox.tsx` is that code, copied until it is released. TanStack Query's paused mutations were considered: they keep no order between writes and have no refused/waiting split. Background Sync needs a secure context. strata-python needed nothing.
- Not done: loading the page itself offline (a service worker; needs https); keeping old packs so a round from them can still be judged after a deploy.

## 2026-11-23: P4 light engagement (0.8.0)

- Asked for: the plan's PR 6: a choice of two activities, the 3-face rating, session caps and breaks as the grown-up's settings, and (from a spec review) a grown-up's overrides of a skill's state (§13, §15). The hesitation and retry patterns the plan lists for P4 (§11) came with it, as an Engagement tab.
- Engagement is kept apart from mastery (§11): ratings and picks never reach the estimates (a unit test rates rounds and gets the same estimates as with no ratings at all). They become one preference per kind, (sum of faces plus half a point per pick, minus half per pass) over (count + 3), so one face moves it a quarter of the way and forty nearly all; it adds 0.3 times that to the enjoyment term. The frustration guard is a filter, not a weight, so no preference can buy a round past it.
- An override is evidence with its source, not an answer: BKT's estimate is set to 0.95 or 0.05 when it is said and moves on with the rounds after it; the rule estimator ignores it, so the two can disagree and the screen shows both. A told skill counts as seen, so the graph does not spread estimates over it, and a "not yet" is a prerequisite gap for what builds on it. Withdrawing keeps the row and its time, so a replay sees what each decision saw.
- Replay: ratings, picks and overrides are stamped under the learner lock and folded as of each decision. The policy is now `utility@2`; with choice and rating off (the defaults in `Config`) it decides exactly as `utility@1`, so every logged decision still replays (a test remakes v1 decisions with v2 code).
- Choices: the second activity is the best candidate of another mechanic (or another kind), so a pick says something about the learner's taste, not about two Counting rounds. Its seed is drawn after the first's, so a replay remakes both. A choice counts as a played round only once picked.
- Patterns are named as hypotheses with what else could explain each (spec §11): quick and right, stuck (many tries or long pauses with few right), fewer right in the second half of longer sessions, mostly sad faces, passed over in picks. Slowness alone is not one: a slow right answer is a learner thinking.

## 2026-11-23: P3 adaptive engine (0.6.0)

- Asked for: the plan's PR 5. The engine is `src/flashtap/engine/`; Play's "Chosen for them" uses it.
- Model: the P2 BKT alone ignores the skill graph, so a new learner would be probed skill by skill. A right answer to a round's primary skill now lifts strong prerequisites with no evidence of their own (to 0.9 of its estimate), and a wrong one caps unseen dependents, which is placement by probes from evidence. A skill's own answers always win over the inference. Prediction multiplies the estimates of every skill a round names, because a Counting round is lost by a learner who cannot read the numeral.
- Simulated learners (`flashtap engine simulate`, 200 learners, 6 sessions, seed 1; fitted by grid search on 140, scored on 60 held out): engine log loss 0.522 (Brier 0.173), BKT without the graph 0.528 (0.175), the rule 0.556 (0.187). The fit lands at prior 0.4 (the top of the grid), learn 0.05, slip 0.12; the rule's best window is 3 or 5. The exit criterion holds on every seed tried (1 to 4: engine 0.490 to 0.522, rule 0.526 to 0.556) and in the unit suite at 40 learners. Most of the gain over the rule is BKT itself; the graph adds a little (0.001 to 0.005), as expected once each skill has answers of its own: it matters most at placement.
- Policy weights: with the learning weight at 1.0 the engine's learners learnt slightly less than under a uniform pick (12.28 against 12.46 skills on 80 learners): enjoyment and frustration pulled it towards easy rounds. At 2.0 they learnt more (12.93 against 12.46, and 12.95 against 12.24 on another seed) at a similar first-answer success rate (0.58). Kept at 2.0; logged with every decision, so a later fit can change it openly.
- Replay: a decision depends on the attempts the server had received and the strikes made by then, so the log now carries each attempt's receipt time and each strike's time (`Event.received_at`, `struck_at`), and a replay folds the log as of the decision's time. Without them a strike made after a session would change every later replay.
- Concurrency (from the first review): receipt times alone do not say what a decision saw, since an attempt stamped before a decision could commit after the decision read the log. Recording an attempt, striking one and making a decision now each take the learner's row lock (`store.hold`, a no-op UPDATE: a row lock on Postgres, the write lock on SQLite) before reading the clock, so time order is commit order per learner; two requests for one decision number queue on the same lock, and a unique (session, seq) backs it up. Play also waits for an answer to be saved before asking for the next decision. A unit test sends answers and next requests at once from threads and replays every decision exactly.
- What the simulation cannot say: its learners follow the model's own assumptions (prerequisites gate learning; one rate per learner), so it shows that the code does what it was designed to, not that a child learns more. That needs real sessions (P4's pilot, P5).

## 2026-11-23: the learner baseline (plan PR 4)

- Asked for: the next PR of the plan, P2: learner records on `strata_python.db`, rule and BKT estimators behind one interface, derived reporting states, an adult Learner screen with evidence provenance, export and delete. Exit: rebuilding from the event log reproduces identical estimates.
- Estimates are not stored at all. The spec's `LearnerEstimate` is a cache, and a cache is the one thing that can disagree with the log; computing the profile from the attempts on each read makes every read a rebuild, and keeps the exit criterion true by construction. It is cheap at this size (a learner's few hundred rounds); a cache can come with P3 if the scheduler needs one, checked against this.
- The server judges the answer. The browser sends the round's (kind, version, choices, seed) and the first answer, never whether it was right; the server rebuilds the round and decides. A version no longer kept is refused rather than guessed at.
- Which attempts count is decided in one place (`evidence.py`), not in each estimator: only the first answer (§10); a wrong answer held back while a confound of the round is unseen (a miscount or a numeral not yet read?), counted once that skill has been shown; secondary and confound skills credited only for a right answer. Every reason is shown beside the attempt on the Learner screen.
- A right answer where a skill was only secondary or a confound moves its estimates, but cannot make it independent: that needs three right answers in rounds written to assess it (§8: a correct multi-skill task is not proof of each prerequisite). Seen in the first run: Counting's numeral confound reached P(known) 0.997 from counting rounds alone and was reported independent; it now stays emerging.
- Independent needs BKT's P(known) of 0.85 and 4 of the last 5 counted answers right (the rule baseline): BKT alone climbed to 0.89 on 5 right of 7 with two misses in the last five.
- Ordering is (presented_at, event_id): attempts queued and sent late, or twice, land in the same place, and the replay test sends the same 60 attempts in shuffled order to a second learner and gets identical reports.
- Delete is a hard delete of every row, explicit child rows first rather than trusting `ON DELETE CASCADE` alone: a SQLite connection without foreign keys on would leave the attempts behind silently. Strike-out (a tombstone) is the correction for one round and keeps the log append-only.
- Session ids come from the browser (one per learner while Play is open), so the server needs no session endpoint until P3's scheduler asks for one. Ids are 32 hex digits from `crypto.getRandomValues`: `crypto.randomUUID` needs a secure context, and the app is often served over plain http on a home network.
- With no database configured the records go in `~/.flashtap/learners.db`, so a checkout or a deployment that has not been given a Postgres schema yet still records. Moving to Postgres does not carry them over.
- strata-react had every component needed (`DataTable` expansion, `useUndoable`, `AlertDialog`, `Status`, `MetricStrip`); nothing was added there. One finding: an `useUndoable` removal committed when its owner unmounts, with no `onError`, rethrows the failure as an unhandled rejection; the tests undo or flush before unmounting, as its docs say.

## 2026-11-23: the mechanics, new kinds of round (plan PR 3)

- Asked for: the next PR of the plan, the eight mechanics with templates that declare their Q-matrix roles and named distractor generators.
- One `Round` shape for both families rather than a type per mechanic: a one-tap round has `options`, a placing round `pieces` and `places`. A place says which pieces it `takes` and how many it `holds`, and the arrangement is right when every place holds exactly that many of them. That one rule is give-N (the basket takes every apple, holds 4), sequence and match (one each), and sort (a group holds all of its pictures); the browser checks with the same rule (`play.ts`), the server's `check` proves it can be met and is met only one way.
- Q-matrix roles are `primary`, `secondary` and `confound`; the spec's own words were not in front of this thread, so these are the plan's reading of §8, kept to three until the learner model (PR 4) needs more. A confound is a skill needed to answer that could explain an error by itself (reading the numeral in Counting, knowing the picture's word in Sort by sound).
- Every wrong option names its generator (`distractor`), so a 9 tapped for six can be read as a reversal later. Near numbers fill at most half of the wrong options, so a round is not always a run of neighbours with the answer in the middle.
- Listen never offers a second letter for the same sound (c and k for /k/), and Sort by sound only pairs sounds spelt by one letter among its pictures: either would make two answers right.
- Mixed now picks the kind and then the round's own seed from the seed it is given. In 0.2 and 0.3 a Mixed round reported its kind and seed, but rebuilding from them gave a different round, because the kind had been drawn from the same generator first.
- The placing board went into strata-react (`PlaceBoard`, PR #285) rather than this repo, following "add components to strata react as needed". It is two taps, pick up then put down, because React Aria's drag and drop starts only from a pointer drag, Enter or a screen reader, never from a tap, and dragging with a small finger (after a long press on iPad) is the harder gesture.

## 2026-11-23: content packs, the P0 ontology seed (plan PR 2)

- Asked for: the next PR of the plan, the content packs for the earliest skills, built to grow through every school year by ability, never by age.
- TOML, not the planned YAML: `tomllib` ships with Python, so the packs add no dependency, and TOML has no implicit typing (YAML reads `no` as false). Pydantic models refuse unknown keys, so a misspelt key fails instead of defaulting.
- Edges are written on either end (`from` or `to`) and turned to one direction in the registry, so a skill's prerequisites sit with the skill. `prerequisite_strong` and `part_of` must be acyclic; `facilitates` may loop (two skills can help each other).
- Confidence is a word (low, medium, high), not a number: these are judgements from guidance, and a probability would claim a precision nobody measured. Edges drafted here, not read from a source, cite `src.flashtap_judgement` so review can start with them.
- The EYFS framework now in force is the one effective 1 September 2026 (dated 13 July 2026). Its maths and literacy goals are 15 statements; 12 map to skills and 3 are set aside because tap rounds cannot observe them (retelling in their own words, forming letters, free writing). Statements that need speech (say a sound, read aloud, count aloud) are mapped, with a note that choosing is the evidence until speech input exists. The statement wording was checked against the PDF only in part (opening words of each bullet).
- `lexicon.py`'s word tables moved into `words.toml` with graphemes resolved to IPA through the phonics inventory. The Letters round now decides "plain first sound" from the first grapheme (one letter) rather than a hand-kept `initial=None`: chicken and cheese drop out for the same reason as before, and no new word can slip in. New decodable picture words (bat, fox, fish, chips...) joined their categories, so Matching, Counting and Letters draw from more pictures.
- Every pack is `draft`: IPA and segmentations were drafted here, and the spec (§9) says generated text must not supply unchecked transcriptions; the Content screen leads with the drafts count until a person reviews them.

## 2026-11-09: web app rebuilt on strata-react

- Asked for: make the template use strata-react fully, as an example and starter for new apps, with Storybook and visual testing.
- The web app is now four screens built only from strata-react (`web/src/screens/`), each opening with its one question and the patterns it shows: Greetings (`useLoad` → `DataView` → `DataTable` with sort in the URL, `RowAction` + `useUndoable` + `UndoBar`, `useSelection` + `BulkBar`, `Field` + `AsyncButton`, `MetricStrip`), Log (`useInfiniteLoad` over a cursor API → `InfiniteList`), System (polled read with `StaleNotice`, `Meter` with thresholds, `CompositionBar`, `Status`, `LinkCard`) and Settings (`Tabs` in the URL, a form started from the server's values, `ColorSchemeChoice`). The hand-written `ui.tsx`, hash router and `useLoad` are gone.
- Routing moved from `#/` hashes to real paths with React Router. To keep one build working behind a reverse proxy at a prefix, the server now answers every non-API path with the app and adds a relative `<base href>` that leads back to its root (`with_base` in `server/app.py`); `api.ts` `BASE` reads it and React Router takes it as `basename`. Checked by hand through a proxy at `/pre/`: deep links, a nested unknown path, sending and removing all worked with no failed request.
- API: `GET /api/system` (`system.py`), `GET /api/messages/page?after=&limit=` (a cursor, not an offset) and `DELETE /api/messages/{id}`. Ids were `len(items) + 1`, which reuses an id once a greeting is removed; they now come from `messages.seq` and are never reused (the web app hides a removed row by id until the removal commits, so a reused id would hide a new greeting).
- Theme: generated by ux-research `palette.mjs` from the subject (postmark ink and stamp red; 42 checks pass); status roles reuse strata-react's fixture values after measuring them on this palette's grounds (every ink at least 6.0:1). Faces are bundled (`@fontsource-variable`), which the visual suite requires.
- Tests: the jsdom suite renders the app's own route tree with `renderRoutes` from `strata-react/testing`, against MSW handlers that now live in `web/src/mocks/` and also serve Storybook and the visual suite. 40 web tests, 100% coverage (floors raised to 100); Python unit coverage 100%.
- Visual: `strata-visual` screenshots every story and the production build's routes (wide and phone) light and dark: 76 baselines, written here in the pinned Chromium (Playwright 1.63.0) and re-run clean. A long, auto-paging log is not screenshotted: which rows are in view depends on when pages land (CI and this machine disagreed on the first run); its paging is covered in jsdom. New CI job `visual` in `unit.yml`.
- Gaps in strata-react, raised as issues rather than worked around: `HttpError` drops the response body, so the server's 400 reason (`detail`) cannot be shown and `api.ts` repeats the server's limits client-side (jburnhams/strata-react#42); `CompositionBar` prints 0% for a part that is small but not zero (jburnhams/strata-react#43).
- One filled button at a time: a review saw the bulk "Remove 2" and "Send greeting" both filled once rows were chosen. The ux-research docs said "one filled per view" and "BulkBar: one filled" without saying which wins; the decision (recorded in ux-research `ui-library/patterns.md` §2 and strata-react's `CLAUDE.md`) is that while rows are chosen the bulk action is the filled one and the screen's primary steps down to outline. `GreetingForm` takes `primary`; the bulk-bar test checks exactly one filled button before, during and after a selection; the four `greetings--selected` baselines changed for it.
